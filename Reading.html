Passage 1

Learning a New Language

Learning a new language can provide many benefits. In an increasingly connected world, people communicate with others from different countries more frequently than ever before. Learning another language can therefore help people communicate more effectively and understand different cultures.

One major advantage of language learning is improved communication. People who speak several languages can communicate with a wider range of people. This can be particularly useful when travelling, studying abroad, or working with international colleagues.

Language is also closely connected to culture. By learning another language, students can discover traditions, customs, ideas and ways of thinking that may be different from their own. This can encourage greater respect and understanding between people.

Another benefit is the possibility of educational and employment opportunities. Some universities offer programmes for students who have language skills. Employers may also value workers who can communicate with international customers and colleagues.

However, learning a language requires patience. Language skills usually develop gradually rather than immediately. Regular reading, writing, listening and speaking can help learners remember vocabulary and become more confident.

Technology can also support language learning. Educational websites, mobile applications, videos and online conversations give learners opportunities to practise outside the classroom.

Making mistakes is a normal part of learning. Instead of becoming discouraged, learners can identify their mistakes, learn from them and try again.

Passage 2

The Changing World of Urban Parks

Urban parks have existed in many cities for centuries, but their purpose has changed considerably. In the past, parks were often designed mainly as attractive spaces where residents could escape crowded streets. Today, many city planners see parks as important parts of healthy and sustainable urban communities.

Modern parks can provide opportunities for exercise. Walking paths, sports fields and outdoor fitness equipment encourage residents to become more physically active. Regular physical activity can contribute to better health and wellbeing.

Parks can also provide environmental benefits. Trees and other plants can help reduce the effects of heat in cities. Vegetation can provide shade and can also contribute to cleaner air. Some modern parks include areas designed to collect rainwater and reduce flooding.

Another important function is social. Parks provide places where people can meet friends, attend community events or simply spend time outdoors. Well-designed public spaces can encourage interaction between people who might otherwise have little opportunity to meet.

However, creating and maintaining parks can be expensive. Cities must pay for landscaping, equipment, cleaning and repairs. Some planners therefore argue that park projects should be designed according to local needs and available resources.

Technology is increasingly being used in park management. Sensors can monitor environmental conditions, while digital systems can help cities understand how different areas are being used. Such information can help planners make better decisions about future improvements.

Passage 3

How Young People Learn

Learning is not limited to classrooms. Young people learn through experiences at home, at school, online and in their communities. Modern research into learning has increasingly focused on how students can become active participants rather than simply receiving information.

One important factor is motivation. Students who understand why a subject is useful may be more willing to spend time learning it. Clear goals can also help students monitor their own progress.

Another factor is the learning environment. A quiet and organised space can make concentration easier, while opportunities to discuss ideas with others can help learners understand difficult concepts from different perspectives.

Technology has changed the way many students study. Online courses, educational videos and interactive exercises can allow students to practise at their own pace. However, technology is most effective when it supports good learning habits rather than simply replacing them.

Memory is also affected by repetition. Reviewing information at different times can help students remember it for longer. Researchers often describe this as spaced practice. Short, regular study sessions may therefore be more useful than attempting to learn everything in one long session.

Mistakes are another important part of learning. When students receive useful feedback, they can understand what went wrong and make changes. Avoiding all mistakes is unrealistic, and treating errors as opportunities to improve can encourage persistence.

Finally, sleep and breaks should not be ignored. Students who study for long periods without rest may find it difficult to maintain concentration. A balanced routine can therefore support both learning and general wellbeing.
